## 3. Best Practices for "Void Fill Only" (No Re-leveling)
Since the driveway is already level, you are filling an erosion gap without forcing the concrete upward. The expanding foam needs a path of least resistance to prevent building localized upward pressure.

* **The "Inside-Out" Pumping Method:** Always start injecting at the deepest, furthest point of the washout and work your way back toward the open edges. This prevents trapping a pocket of foam that could build up localized pressure and crack or lift the center of the driveway.
* **Using Short 3-Second Bursts:** Use short, controlled 2-to-3-second bursts of foam rather than a continuous spray. This allows the material time to expand laterally into the washout space without creating an immediate upward surge.
* **Adjacent Holes as Sight Glasses:** Drill injection ports roughly 2 to 3 feet apart. Leave the adjacent holes completely unplugged. When you see foam begin to squeeze up out of a neighboring hole or the open side, that zone is full, signaling you to move to the next port.

## 4. Finishing, Landscaping & UV Protection

### Handling the "Blop Out"
As you fill the edge, foam will naturally travel out from under the slab.
* **Do not touch it while it is wet:** Polyurethane foam is incredibly sticky and contains chemicals that are difficult to remove from bare skin and clothing.
* **Let it over-expand:** Do not try to smooth or scrape it flush while it is in its liquid or gel state. Let it expand into "loaves" past the edge of the driveway and allow it to cure completely. It is far easier to manage once it is solid.

### Trimming the Excess Cleanly
Because lifting foam is dense, closed-cell polyurethane, it cures into a rigid, tough plastic.
* **The Best Tools:** A standard utility knife blade is usually too short and will catch. Instead, use a **long serrated bread knife**, a **hand saw**, or a **reciprocating saw (Sawzall)** with a long, flexible construction blade.
* **The Technique:** Press the flat side of the blade flush against the vertical concrete edge of your driveway. Use a sawing motion to slice right through the foam "loaves". This will leave a clean, flat foam profile perfectly flush with the edge of your concrete slab.

### Critical Step: UV Protection
* **Polyurethane foam degrades rapidly under sunlight.** Within a few weeks of direct UV exposure, the foam will turn a deep orange/brown, become brittle, and begin to powder and dust away.
* To prevent this from ruining your structural fill, you must coat or cover the exposed face. If you are using soil/landscaping to cover it up completely, that acts as a natural shield. If any foam will remain above-ground or near the surface, paint the trimmed face with an **exterior latex paint** or a UV-resistant protective coating.

### Landscaping Re-entry
Once the foam is flush-trimmed and protected, you can rebuild the landscape profile:
* **Create a Barrier:** Before throwing dirt back against the foam, you can lay down a strip of heavy-duty **landscape fabric** against the foam face. This helps keep moisture and roots from interacting directly with the edge.
* **Backfill and Grade:** Tamp down fresh topsoil or gravel tightly against the driveway edge to seal off the void permanently. Slope the dirt slightly *away* from the driveway edge so that future rainwater runoff flows into the yard instead of channeling right back underneath the concrete to cause a secondary washout.
